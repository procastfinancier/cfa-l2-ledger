TITLE: Forwards - Price, Arbitrage and Value (R28)

AVA: Welcome to the CFA Ledger Podcast, Derivatives. Short episodes, one example per formula, and a memory hook at the end. I'm Ava.
ANDREW: And I'm Andrew. Episode one: forward contracts.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: What is a forward?
ANDREW: An agreement today to buy or sell an asset later, at a price fixed today. The buyer is long, the seller is short. The long gains if the spot price at expiry is above the forward price; the short gains if it is below. Nothing is paid at the start, so the contract's value is zero at inception. And note: a forward has no exercise price. That's option language.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: How is the forward price set?
ANDREW: Cost of carry. The seller buys the asset today with borrowed money and holds it. So the forward price equals spot, minus the present value of benefits like dividends or coupons, plus the present value of costs like storage, all grown at the risk-free rate. F equals S minus PV of benefits plus PV of costs, times one plus r to the power t.
AVA: Example.
ANDREW: A share at 500 rupees, rate 6 percent, three months, no dividends. F equals 500 times 1.06 to the power 0.25, which is 507.34.
AVA: And if the market quotes 510?
ANDREW: The forward is overpriced, so do cash-and-carry. Borrow 500, buy the share, sell the forward at 510. At expiry, deliver for 510 and repay 507.34. Profit 2.66. If the forward were cheap, say 502, reverse it: short the share, lend the 500, buy the forward. Profit 507.34 minus 502, which is 5.34.
ANDREW: This assumes no transaction costs, unrestricted short selling, and borrowing and lending at the risk-free rate.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Which compounding do we use?
ANDREW: Three cases. Rate-based contracts like FRAs, swaps, caps and floors: simple interest on a 360-day year. Stocks, bonds and currencies: discrete compounding on 365 days. Equity indexes: continuous compounding on 365 days. To convert, the continuous rate is the natural log of one plus the discrete rate. Five percent annual is 4.879 percent continuous.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Now a dividend-paying stock.
ANDREW: Price 30. Dividends of 0.40 on day 15, 0.40 on day 85, and 0.50 on day 175. A 100-day forward at 5 percent. The trap: the day 175 dividend falls after expiry, so ignore it. F equals 30 minus the PV of the two 0.40 dividends, which is 29.21, times 1.05 to the power 100 over 365. That gives 29.60.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: What is the value of a forward after inception?
ANDREW: Value is simply today's gain or loss. For the long: today's spot minus the PV of remaining dividends, minus the PV of the original forward price. Sixty days later the stock is at 36. One dividend remains, in 25 days, worth 0.40 divided by 1.05 to the power 25 over 365. So 36 minus that is 35.60. The PV of 29.60 over the 40 remaining days is 29.44. Value to the long is 35.60 minus 29.44, which is 6.16. The short's value is minus 6.16.
ANDREW: If today's forward price is given instead, the value is the new forward price minus the old one, discounted to today. One more trap: if an analyst forgets dividends when valuing a short forward, the short is undervalued by the PV of the dividends.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Memory hook.
ANDREW: Forward equals spot plus carry. Add costs, subtract benefits. Sell the expensive one, buy the cheap one. Dividends after expiry don't count. Value is today's profit, discounted.
