TITLE: Interest Rate and Currency Swaps (R28)

AVA: Episode three. Swaps.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: What is an interest rate swap?
ANDREW: One side pays fixed, the other pays floating, on a notional that is never exchanged. Only the net interest moves. Think of turning a floating-rate home loan into a fixed one. Floating payments are set at the start of each period and paid at the end. That's called settled in arrears. On a reset date, the floating leg is worth par, because its coupon equals the market rate.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: How is a swap priced?
ANDREW: Pricing means finding the fixed rate that makes the swap worth zero at the start, so the PV of the fixed leg equals the PV of the floating leg. The result: the periodic swap rate equals one minus the last discount factor, divided by the sum of the discount factors. Each discount factor Z is one over, one plus the rate times days over 360.
AVA: Example.
ANDREW: A one-year quarterly swap on 50 lakh. Rates of 3, 3.5, 4 and 4.5 percent give Z of 0.99256, 0.98280, 0.97087 and 0.95694. Sum, 3.9032. One minus 0.95694 is 0.04306. Divide by 3.9032 to get 1.1 percent a quarter, or 4.4 percent a year. The quarterly fixed payment is 50 lakh times 0.044 times 0.25, which is 55,000.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Who gains when rates move?
ANDREW: Rates up, the fixed payer gains: they pay the old low rate and receive the new higher floating rate. Rates down, the fixed receiver gains. The value to the fixed payer equals the new swap rate minus the old one, times days over 360, times notional, times the sum of the remaining discount factors. The exam asks for interest rate swap values only on settlement dates.
AVA: Example.
ANDREW: A two-year swap at 6.62 percent on 10 lakh. After 180 days the new rate is 5.8 percent, and the remaining Z sum to 2.8478. Point zero five eight minus 0.0662 is minus 0.0082. Times 0.5, times 10 lakh, times 2.8478, is about minus 11,662. Rates fell, so the fixed payer has lost and pays the receiver.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: And currency swaps?
ANDREW: Three differences. Two yield curves and two fixed rates, one per currency. Principal really is exchanged, at the start and at the end, sized at the starting exchange rate. And when you value it, convert the foreign leg at today's spot rate.
ANDREW: Example: an Indian company borrowed 5 million dollars. At 84 rupees per dollar, the rupee notional is 42 crore. The dollar swap rate is 4.4 percent; the rupee curve gives about 6.8 percent. Each quarter it receives 55,000 dollars and pays 71.4 lakh rupees. Value equals the PV of what you receive minus the PV of what you pay. Later the rupee weakens to 87, the dollar leg is worth more, and the company gains about 153,674 dollars. That's the hedge working.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Memory hook.
ANDREW: Floating equals par on reset. Swap rate equals one minus last Z over sum of Z. Rates up, payer wins. Currency swap: two curves, real principal, today's spot.
