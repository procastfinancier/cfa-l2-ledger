TITLE: Equity Swaps and Bond Futures (R28)

AVA: Episode four. Equity swaps and bond futures.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: What's special about an equity swap?
ANDREW: One leg pays the return on a stock or index. If the other leg is fixed, it is priced exactly like an interest rate swap, with the same formula. The difference: equity swaps can be valued between settlement dates. The equity leg equals notional times, one plus the return since the last reset.
AVA: Example.
ANDREW: One crore notional, fixed rate 6.05 percent. Nifty moves from 24,625 to 24,900 in 30 days. The return is 275 divided by 24,625, which is 1.12 percent. The equity leg is one crore times 1.0112, which is 1 crore 1 lakh 11 thousand 675. The fixed leg's PV is 99 lakh 40 thousand. The value to the fixed payer is the difference, 1 lakh 71 thousand 675.
AVA: And swapping one stock's return for another?
ANDREW: There's no pricing at the start. The value is the return received minus the return paid, times notional. Pay Infosys, receive TCS, on 10 lakh. Infosys is up 1.3 percent and TCS is down 0.8 percent. Minus 0.013 minus 0.008 is minus 0.021. Times 10 lakh, that's minus 21,000. You lose on both legs.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Now bond futures. Clean versus dirty price?
ANDREW: Accrued interest equals days since the last coupon, over days between coupons, times the coupon. The clean price is quoted without it. The dirty price, or full price, is clean plus accrued interest. That's what the buyer actually pays.
AVA: What's the delivery option?
ANDREW: The short may deliver any of several eligible bonds, so the short holds a valuable option and will deliver the cheapest to deliver. Each bond has a conversion factor, and the long pays the futures price times that factor.
AVA: The formula?
ANDREW: Quoted futures price equals one over the conversion factor, times: full price, times one plus r to the power T, minus the future value of coupons received, minus accrued interest at expiry. Example: bond price 1,040, conversion factor 1.13, 5 percent, 1.2 years, future value of coupons 71.56, accrued interest at expiry 14. 1,040 times 1.05 to the 1.2 is 1,102.71. Minus 71.56, minus 14, gives 1,017.15. Divided by 1.13, that's 900.13.
AVA: And arbitrage?
ANDREW: If the fair quoted price is 133.65 and the market shows 135, the futures are rich. Sell the futures and buy the bond. The profit at expiry is 135 minus 133.65, times the conversion factor of 0.8, about 1.08.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Memory hook, and Reading 28 in one line.
ANDREW: Equity leg equals notional times one plus return. Stock for stock: received minus paid. The short picks the cheapest bond. Divide by the conversion factor. And all of Reading 28 is no arbitrage: forward equals spot plus carry, an FRA is a forward on a rate, and a swap is a bundle of FRAs.
