TITLE: Black-Scholes-Merton, the Black Model and Swaptions (R29)

AVA: Episode seven. Black-Scholes-Merton and its variations.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: The assumptions.
ANDREW: BSM is the binomial idea in continuous time. Returns are lognormal, so prices move smoothly with no jumps. The risk-free rate and volatility are known and constant. Options are European. There are no frictions, trading is continuous, and short selling is allowed. A constant yield on the underlying is allowed, so "the underlying has no cash flows" is not an assumption. That's a classic trap.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: What raises an option's value?
ANDREW: A higher stock price raises calls and lowers puts. A higher strike does the opposite. A higher rate raises calls and lowers puts. Higher volatility and more time raise both. Dividends lower calls and raise puts.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: The formula.
ANDREW: Call equals S times N of d1, minus the PV of X times N of d2. Put equals the PV of X times N of minus d2, minus S times N of minus d1. So a call is a leveraged stock position: buy N of d1 shares and borrow. A put is a long bond plus a short stock position. N of d1 is the call's delta. N of d2 is the risk-neutral probability the call ends in the money. N of d1 plus N of minus d1 equals one.
AVA: Example.
ANDREW: Stock 50, strike 45, half a year, rate 3 percent, N of d1 0.779, N of d2 0.723. The PV of X is 45 times e to the minus 0.015, which is 44.33. The call is 50 times 0.779 minus 44.33 times 0.723: 38.95 minus 32.05 is 6.90. If the market price is 7, sell the call and buy the replicating portfolio for a 10-paise profit.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Variations.
ANDREW: Same recipe, just change S. With dividends, use S times e to the minus delta T. For currencies, use S times e to the minus the foreign rate times T, because the foreign rate acts like a dividend yield. The Black model, for options on futures: replace S with the PV of F. There's no r in d1, because F already includes carry.
ANDREW: Interest rate options: use the FRA rate in place of F, and multiply by the accrual period and the notional. Discount from the payment date, at the end of the FRA period, and use actual over 365.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Swaptions?
ANDREW: A payer swaption is the right to pay fixed, so it's exercised if swap rates rise. A receiver swaption is the right to receive fixed, exercised if rates fall. The formula replaces the FRA rate with the swap rate, and the discount factor with the PVA, the sum of the swap's discount factors.
[PAUSE 1]
AVA: Memory hook.
ANDREW: Call equals S N d1 minus PV of X N d2. N d1 is delta; N d2 is probability. Dividends, currency, futures, rates, swaps: same recipe, change the S. Payer swaption wins when rates rise.
